    The problem

    Drug screening still runs on human hands.

    Most labs that screen every week (biotech startups, academic labs, core facilities, small CROs) sit between pipetting by hand and automation they can't afford.

    Manual dilutions and pipetting

    Scientists calculate dilution series and pipette hundreds of wells by hand, plate after plate.

    One wrong concentration costs a week

    A single error in the maths or a skipped well can ruin a whole screen, and it often shows up only in the readout.

    Automation built for big pharma

    Full screening platforms cost six figures and need automation engineers to program every run.

    Our approach

    Computational AI plans. Physical AI executes.

    Neither is useful alone. Athelas connects the two, with the scientist in the loop.

    Computational AI

    A model that can't touch the lab

    …stays a suggestion. Plans, concentrations and plate layouts are only worth something once they are run.

    Physical AI

    A robot without a plan

    …automates mistakes. Speed is only useful if every transfer has been planned and checked first.

    Athelas: the plan, the checks and the robot, in one loop.
    How it works

    From experiment to robot run in four steps.

    Plan

    Choose compounds, doses and controls in the browser. Athelas works out every volume and dilution.

    Check

    An independent checker replays the plan and catches the mistakes that really happen at the bench.

    Approve

    A scientist reviews and signs off. Humans stay at the helm; the robot never runs unapproved.

    Run

    An affordable liquid handler executes the plan, and every transfer is logged.

    Origin

    Born at a physical AI hackathon in San Francisco.

    The idea started with a lab tool built by a scientist who ran the screens herself: a Drug Concentration Calculator, born out of years of manual dilution maths. In one weekend, it grew into a robot that runs the experiment.
